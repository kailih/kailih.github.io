I work on post-training and reinforcement learning for large language models. The parts I care about most are the ones that decide whether a model actually got better or only looks like it did: the reward that says what good means, the data the policy learns from, the evaluation that would catch it lying, and the training loop that holds the three together. At Apple I do this for LLM retrieval agents, from SFT through GRPO; I recently co-developed CoGR, which trains two LLMs to co-evolve against a single shared retrieval reward.

I got here by way of dialogue. At Tsinghua I co-created the Chinese dialogue datasets people still train on: CrossWOZ , KdConv , and LCCC , which six years on still sees on Hugging Face; at Stanford I built retrieval systems and tried model-based RL on dialogue policy for the first time; at ByteDance AI Lab I built and deployed fake-news and harmful-content detectors, from the annotation guidelines behind 2M+ human labels to automated retraining; at Microsoft AI I built the evaluation and alignment loop behind a retrieval-augmented Copilot. Each stop was a different piece of the same loop: the data a model learns from, the reward and training that make it better, and the check that it actually did.

Thinking about: rewards that come from the real task rather than a proxy of it, how far an experiment loop can be handed to an agent before you stop trusting the result, and what an assistant has to understand about a person before it can take real weight off their mind, not just off their calendar. Chasing the same questions? Say hi.

ideas I keep returning to

Agents that run their own experiments
An agent proposes, trains, evaluates, and decides what to try next, inside rules a person sets.
what it taught me about agency

Today's agents are good at pushing forward. What I want from the next generation is the judgment to stop: to step back and ask whether the whole approach is right, to change the architecture rather than the next line, and to reason from first principles: the goal is not to solve every problem it is handed, nor to grind on a particular one, but to keep the capacity to ask which problem is the right one. Most of my rule-setting in the experiment loop is an attempt to supply that judgment from the outside.

A second brain for everyone
AI moves from a chatbot you visit to something you carry. It takes the tedious work off your mind, so you have the headspace to think and decide better.
the longer bet

This is a long bet, measured in decades rather than product cycles. The hard parts are three: model capability, latency low enough that the system feels like part of you rather than a tool you wait on, and the interaction itself, how such a thing is built and wired into a life. It does the arithmetic, looks things up seamlessly as the conversation happens, remembers what you would otherwise have to memorize, helps you plan your life without the overhead, and clears away the tedious work that fills a day. You still do the thinking and make the decisions. It just makes sure nothing trivial is in the way. The measure of such a system is not how smart it sounds but how much clearer the person thinks, and how much better they decide, with it than without it. Underneath all of it is a simple belief: AI should serve people. Being free to do what you love and create what you want to create is one of the most valuable things a person could have. A system like this is worth building if it gives more people more of that freedom.

selected projects

CoGR: Co-Evolving Generative Retrieval

Two LLMs, one for queries and one for items, learn to write matching keyword representations. After SFT they are trained with alternating GRPO updates against a single shared retrieval objective, with a counterfactual marginal reward for item-level credit assignment. 10.9–36.1% relative F1 gains over the strongest baseline.

Autoresearch: an agent that runs the experiment loop

A generic workflow in which an LLM agent proposes, trains, evaluates, and accepts or rejects experiments on its own, inside rules a person sets. I built one instance of it for model compression; after manual validation of the first iterations, it ran a multi-day sequence of experiments without further input and delivered a materially smaller model within the performance tolerance.

ColBERT-Serve

A serving system that memory-maps the ColBERT index, uses a sparse first stage (SPLADEv2) to pick candidates, and re-ranks them with ColBERT under a hybrid score. Cuts RAM by 90% and lets late-interaction retrieval run on inexpensive servers under many concurrent queries. ECIR 2025, with Matei Zaharia (Databricks co-founder), Christopher Potts, et al.
